# Per-Bus LMP Validation Report — Phase A.2

Generated by `experiments/per_bus_lmp_validation/run.py`

## Methodology

Compared model bus-level LMPs from cached Vatic simulations (3-day
September windows in 2020, 2022, 2023) against NYISO published
day-ahead LBMPs at both generator-node and zonal levels.

- **17 of 35 buses** have direct nodal matches to NYISO generator PTIDs.
- **18 buses** use zone-average LBMP as the reference (no direct match).

See `docs/nygrid_to_nyiso_node_mapping.md` for the complete mapping.

Note: These results predate the Phase A.1 multi-bus import fix.
The model used in these simulations had single-bus imports and the
original (slightly inaccurate) Kron-reduced JSON.

## Key Finding: PJM Import Cost Dominates LMP Distribution

The model's LMP distribution is heavily bimodal:

| LMP range | 2020 | 2023 |
|-----------|-----:|-----:|
| $28-32 (PJM marginal) | **56%** of bus-hours | **43%** of bus-hours |
| $5-20 (internal marginal) | 41% | 26% |

When PJM is the marginal source (importing at $30/MWh), all bus LMPs
converge to approximately $30 with minimal spatial variation. This
causes:

1. **Positive bias** (+$4-8/MWh): The model dispatches PJM imports at
   a fixed $30/MWh, but NYISO's actual DA prices in September 2020 and
   2023 averaged $15-23. The PJM import cost is too high relative to
   the actual market clearing price.

2. **Low correlation**: When 43-56% of hours have uniform LMP at $30,
   the model cannot track the hourly price variation that NYISO
   exhibits. NYISO prices respond to load shapes, wind availability,
   and strategic bidding — none of which affect the model when PJM is
   setting the price.

Errors are much larger (MAE $41). The root cause is the known import

$60-80/MWh. This is exactly what Phase A.3 (Path B import pricing)
addresses.

model imports cheap HQ power while reality had much higher prices.

## Assessment

### What Works

- **Bus-level LMP spatial variation** exists and is directionally
  correct: downstate (zones G-K) is more expensive than upstate (A-F).
- **Congestion effects** are present: zones J, K have higher LMPs
  than zones A, B, C.
- **2023 meets the $10/MWh threshold** with median AE = $7.25.
- **2020 is borderline** at $9.06 — close to passing.

### What Doesn't Work

- **Hourly correlation is poor** (0.23-0.51 vs. 0.75 target). This
  is structural: cost-based dispatch cannot replicate market-cleared
  price dynamics. The model needs imports to be marginal less often.
- **PJM import at $30/MWh is too high** for these September shoulder
  periods, creating a bias floor.
- **2022 is unacceptable** without year-specific import pricing.

### Root Cause

The import cost structure drives everything:
- 43-56% of hours have LMP = PJM cost ($30), regardless of actual
  NYISO market conditions
- HQ at $5/MWh is too cheap for 2022 (should be ~$48)
- The model cannot reproduce hourly price dynamics when a single
  import source dominates the marginal price

**This is exactly what Phase A.3 (Path B import pricing) is designed
to fix.** Year-specific import costs will:
1. Reduce the frequency of PJM-marginal hours
2. Correct the HQ pricing for high-gas years
3. Improve the bias and potentially the correlation


## Go/No-Go Recommendation

**CONDITIONAL GO** — Proceed to Phase A.3 (Path B import pricing).

The per-bus LMP validation reveals that import pricing is the binding
constraint on model accuracy, confirming the need for Phase A.3. The
underlying grid model (topology, impedances, generator costs) is sound:
- Spatial LMP patterns are correct
- Intra-zone spread is realistic
- Errors are systematically explained by import pricing

Phase A.3 should directly address the $30/MWh PJM ceiling and the
$5/MWh HQ floor that dominate the LMP distribution.
